# JutJut business UX/product audit

**Perspective:** Business client: hiring employer, Drop advertiser, and practicals host  
**Scope:** Current preview and implemented source only; no product changes made  
**Audit date:** 17 September 2026

## Executive conclusion

JutJut has a credible **operational core** for three business outcomes: verified-student job listings, reviewed student offers (“Drops”), and institution-approved practical opportunities. The implemented dashboards contain useful foundations, including paid job-post credits, job and Drop performance metrics, review-status visibility, institution approval gates, and QR redemption records. The key risk is not a lack of concepts; it is a mismatch between the public promise, the paid journey, and the controls a business needs to realise and prove value.

For a prospective business, the landing page repeatedly promises immediate actions such as **“post a job,” “start free trial,”** and **“contact sales,”** but each current public pricing action leads only to the generic waitlist. Once signed in, the job, Drop, and practicals workflows live in separate areas with limited cross-navigation. The result is a difficult-to-understand offer: a business cannot readily tell what it can do now, what it will pay, who decides, when it will be live, or how success translates into hires or actual redemptions. Addressing these clarity and measurement gaps within the present dashboard model should be the first priority.

## What is working

- **The business proposition has real operational substance.** The employer area supports profile setup, a credit balance and history, job posting, payment gateway feedback, expiry, and auto-repost. The Drop area supports submission, review status, branding, and analytics. The practicals workflow records business need, deliverables, named supervision, safety acknowledgement, institutional review, milestones, and completion approval. [2] [3] [4]
- **Approval and safety boundaries are unusually clear in Practicals.** Businesses cannot host without an active business profile. Students cannot use an opportunity before institution approval, and the institution retains final completion approval after supervisor confirmation. This is a strong credibility foundation for course-linked work. [4] [9]
- **Early-state guidance is mostly thoughtful.** The dashboards use loading feedback and actionable empty states, for example “Submit your first Drop,” “Post your first job,” and “Create opportunity.” A zero-credit state explains the constraint and provides a Buy Credits action. [2] [3] [4]
- **The implemented analytics are more substantive than the landing page indicates.** Job detail includes views, applications, hires, conversion, time to first application, average verified skills, applicant details, school breakdown, and trend data. Drop detail includes impressions, claims, spend, claim rate, cost per impression/claim, and audience breakdowns. [2] [3] [5]
- **Sensitive student contact is handled with a consent-aware pattern.** The employer-facing applicant table can show a student as anonymous and contact as hidden, and the server snapshots the student’s contact-sharing preference at application time. [2] [5]

## Prioritised findings

| Priority | Finding | Evidence | Recommendation | Expected impact |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Critical** | **Public employer calls to action do not reach an employer action or sales path.** | In the live preview, “I’m hiring – post a job” scrolls to the employer marketing section; “Start hiring verified students” scrolls to pricing; and all three price-card actions—including “Contact sales”—scroll to the generic waitlist. The waitlist defaults to Student and collects only name/email, with no business context or stated next step. [1] [6] | Keep the waitlist if launch-gated, but give employer actions an employer-specific endpoint/state: preselect **Employer**, explain that access is launch-gated, collect business name and intended use (hire, Drop, practical), and set a concrete follow-up expectation. Rename CTAs to “Join employer waitlist” until direct activation exists. | Removes a high-friction acquisition dead end, improves lead qualification, and makes funnel conversion measurable by business use case. |
| **Critical** | **Published pricing and actual monetisation/entitlements conflict.** | The landing page advertises Starter/Growth/Scale subscriptions ($0/$49/$129) and promises tier-specific features such as analytics, verified-only filtering, branding, and API access. The implemented employer transaction is instead a $15 single credit or $50 five-credit purchase; analytics are rendered for all employer profiles, and no subscription/tier enforcement appears in the source search. [2] [5] [6] | Select the monetisation model already closest to launch and make every surface consistent. If credits are the live model, present clear per-post pricing and remove or label subscription benefits as future. If plans are intended, implement plan state and entitlement gates before marketing them. Do not route “free trial” to an unspecified waitlist. | Protects pricing trust, prevents support and refund friction, and enables valid revenue, conversion, and plan-adoption reporting. |
| **High** | **The product splits a business’s work across dashboards without a clear information architecture.** | Job management is in **Employer Dashboard**; offers and Drop analytics are in **Business Dashboard**; practicals are a third workspace. The signed-in navbar’s business-specific profile item is only “Drop Analytics”; it has no direct Employer Dashboard item, while the principal nav is student-oriented. [2] [3] [7] [8] | Add a clearly named **Business** entry in the existing signed-in navigation, with links/cards for Jobs, Drops, Practicals, performance, and account settings. Keep the current underlying pages; make the navigation and terms consistent rather than redesigning the workflows. | Reduces navigation hunting and makes cross-sell between hiring, local promotion, and practicals realistic. |
| **High** | **Drop submission does not collect the operational inputs implied by the sales promise.** | The form captures title, free-text description, maximum claims, preferred date, and optional image. Yet the interface says JutJut will set the fee and schedule the Drop for the business’s “target student audience”; no audience, location, school/year targeting, redemption method, offer terms, or budget input is structured. [3] | Add only the missing structured fields already implied by the current workflow: target geography/school preference, intended audience, redemption instructions/terms, and commercial constraints. If JutJut deliberately controls targeting and price, state this directly and show those decisions as separate review checkpoints in My Drops. | Produces review-ready submissions, shortens clarification cycles, and creates valid segmentation fields for post-campaign measurement. |
| **High** | **ROI reporting measures claims, not the in-store outcome businesses are funding.** | The Drop dashboard reports impressions, claims, claim rate, cost per impression, cost per claim, spend, and audience breakdowns. However, the schema and redemption handler already record a claim’s `redeemedAt` through QR redemption, while the business analytics surface does not show redeemed offers, claim-to-redemption rate, or cost per redemption. [3] [10] | Add **Claims**, **Redeemed**, **redemption rate**, and **cost per redemption** to the existing Drop summary/detail. Label “claim” distinctly from “redeem,” and show zero/partial data with an explanatory note. This uses existing redemption records rather than requiring external point-of-sale integration. | Lets businesses assess actual footfall/offer use, rather than treating intent as ROI; substantially strengthens renewal and sponsorship conversations. |
| **High** | **An employer can see applicant data but cannot complete a normal hiring workflow.** | The job detail panel shows applicant identity/contact when consented, skill count, and status, but supplies no shortlist, decline, contact-log, interview, or hired action. The employer router exposes posting, viewing, analytics, and student application only; there is no employer application-status mutation. Yet the landing page promises employers can see who “was hired,” and the analytics detail includes Hires. [2] [5] | Add lightweight employer-side actions for the statuses already displayed—at minimum shortlist, decline, and hired—plus a confirmation state and student notification. Retain privacy safeguards and make any hire metric derive from that documented workflow. | Closes the conversion loop from applicant evidence to hiring outcome and makes job conversion/hire KPIs credible. |
| **Medium** | **Practical opportunity submission is operationally rich but unnecessarily hard to complete and recover from.** | The host dialog presents a long, scrollable form with roughly 17 inputs. The client-side precheck only verifies pathway and safety acknowledgement; the server applies stricter requirements for scope, business need, deliverables, supervisor email, cadence, and course areas. Errors surface as a toast rather than near the relevant field. [4] [9] | Preserve the current compliance/safety data but group it into short sections—opportunity, supervision, and conditions—mark server-required fields before submit, and show inline errors/focus the first invalid field. Add a non-submitting “Save as draft” only if it can reuse the existing draft status safely. | Improves completion quality, reduces failed submission retries, and protects the institution review queue from underspecified briefs. |
| **Medium** | **A practicals access failure is presented as a profile-setup problem, obscuring recovery.** | `BusinessPracticals` renders the same “Set up your business profile first” card for any query error or missing data. The server distinguishes no profile, inactive profile, and other errors, including a message for a profile that is not active. [4] [9] | Branch the existing error state: show setup guidance only for the no-profile condition; show inactive-status context and a contact/recovery path for suspended profiles; show retry/support for network or server errors. | Avoids misleading a legitimate business, shortens support resolution, and makes account status operationally legible. |
| **Medium** | **The launch marketing has preventable accessibility and trust gaps.** | The viewport meta tag sets `maximum-scale=1`, which restricts zoom. The visible waitlist inputs rely on placeholders rather than associated labels; first name has `aria-required` but no programmatic label. FAQ buttons do not expose expanded state. Footer “Privacy,” “Terms,” and “Contact” each scroll to the waitlist instead of a policy/contact destination. The footer also displays a placeholder-format ABN (`00 000 000 000`) beside strong privacy claims. [6] [11] | Remove the zoom cap, add visible `<label>` elements and `aria-expanded`/`aria-controls`, and retain the existing inline validation/error alerts. Replace nonfunctional legal/contact buttons with real destinations or label them as forthcoming; do not present placeholder legal/business details as final. | Improves keyboard, screen-reader, and low-vision access while removing credibility objections at the point of commercial consideration. |

## Quick wins for the next implementation cycle

1. Change all launch-gated business CTAs to **Join employer waitlist** and preselect Employer; add business name and intended use, plus an explicit follow-up message.
2. Reconcile the public pricing cards with the implemented $15/$50 credit model, or visibly mark plan features as not yet available.
3. Add a **Business** nav entry that exposes Jobs, Drops, Practicals, and Settings without requiring a profile-menu discovery step.
4. Surface **redeemed offers**, redemption rate, and cost per redemption from existing QR redemption data; clearly distinguish claims from redemptions.
5. Add employer controls for shortlist/decline/hired and feed those status changes into the existing job analytics.
6. Add required markers and inline server-validation feedback to the existing Practical Opportunity form; do not reduce the supervision/safety requirements.
7. Correct practicals error branching, remove `maximum-scale=1`, label the waitlist fields, and make FAQ state programmatically available.
8. Link Privacy, Terms, and Contact to actual content or label them as unavailable before taking paid business leads.

## Measurement plan

The present implementation can support a compact business scorecard without new external integrations. Track: employer CTA → employer waitlist completion → account/profile completion → first job/Drop/practical submitted → review approval → live campaign/listing; then track job view-to-application and application-to-hired rate, plus Drop impression-to-claim and claim-to-redemption rate. Record the exact implementation date for any pricing change so marketing conversion and paid conversion can be interpreted against the correct offer.
